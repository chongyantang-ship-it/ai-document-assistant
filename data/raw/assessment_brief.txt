Assessment 3: Real World Applications of Artificial Intelligence

Overview:
Assessment 3 is a group project with a report and an oral presentation. It is worth 40% and focuses on applying AI techniques to solve a real-world problem. The report is due on 16 May 2026 at 23:59. The oral presentation is due in Week 12 for Group A on 7 May 2026 and in Week 13 for Group B on 14 May 2026.

Purpose:
In Assessment 3, students focus on how to apply AI techniques to solve real-world problems, showcasing theoretical knowledge, collaboration, and practical implementation.

Learning Outcomes:
SLO1 Understand general problem-solving approaches in AI.
SLO2 Apply knowledge of logic and structural knowledge representation.
SLO4 Multilayer networks, reinforcement learning, Generative AI, and LLM.
SLO5 Explore real-world applications of AI considering AI ethics and responsible AI design.

Type:
Report and Oral Presentation.

Groupwork:
This is a group assignment with individually assessed contributions. The maximum group size is six students.

Weighting:
40%.

Length:
Up to 3500 words, including references. Use 11-point Times or Arial fonts.

Similarity Checks:
Similarity checks on the report will be done by Turnitin. Multiple submissions are allowed. The maximum allowed similarity score is 20%, including quoted material and excluding references. Marks may be lost if the similarity score is higher than 20%.

Submission Format:
Report submission: PDF. Only one member from the group needs to submit the report. The first page must clearly list all group members' names and student IDs. The PowerPoint slide file must also be submitted along with the report submission.

Presentation Session:
The presentation is 8 minutes in total, including a 7-minute presentation plus 1-minute Q&A. The PowerPoint file should be submitted before the class presentation session.

Submission Due:
Report due in Week 13 on 16 May 2026.
Presentation due in Week 12 for Group A on 7 May 2026.
Presentation due in Week 13 for Group B on 14 May 2026.

Task Overview:
This is a group assignment. While the project is collaborative, individual contributions will be assessed separately. Each group member must clearly document their contributions at the end of the report. The assessment reflects postgraduate-level expectations and requires application of AI techniques, critical evaluation, comparative analysis, and research-informed decision-making.

Objective:
Identify a real-world problem that can be effectively addressed using artificial intelligence techniques. The solution must go beyond software development and demonstrate integration of multiple AI paradigms such as logic, structural knowledge representation, predictive modelling, probabilistic reasoning, multi-layer networks, reinforcement learning, GenAI, or LLM, together with strong theoretical justification, rigorous empirical evaluation, and critical reflection on limitations, trade-offs, and scalability.

Example Domains:
Example domains include intelligent document processing such as CV parsing plus LLM-based querying, decision support systems, recommendation systems, and autonomous agents or optimisation problems. Students are encouraged to be creative, but the solution must centre on AI techniques rather than simply on software development.

Part 1 Report:
The report is worth 30 marks.

Report Sections:
The report must include the following sections:
Introduction. Clearly define the real-world problem the group aims to address. Explain its real-world significance and impact, identify key challenges and constraints, clearly state project objectives and scope, and explain the benefits of an effective solution.
Theoretical Justifications. Provide a detailed explanation of the proposed AI-based method. Justify why the methods are appropriate for the problem and support the argument with relevant academic literature.
Workflow and Empirical Analysis. Describe the workflow of the developed solution, including key steps such as data preprocessing, algorithmic approach, and implementation details. Explain model design, algorithmic choices, and integration of multiple AI paradigms. Clearly distinguish design decisions from implementation details. Present experimental results using either real-world datasets sourced online or synthetic data created by the group. Include empirical analysis that demonstrates the effectiveness of the solution. Describe the experimental setup, use appropriate evaluation metrics, present results clearly using tables or figures, and provide a link to a publicly accessible GitHub repository containing the full code implementation. The report should focus on describing the workflow rather than including raw code.
Critical Reflection. Critically evaluate limitations and failure cases of the approach, trade-offs such as optimality versus efficiency or accuracy versus interpretability, scalability, and computational constraints, and when alternative approaches may be more suitable.
Additional Expectations. Use academic references where appropriate, demonstrate critical thinking rather than just implementation, clearly distinguish design choices from results, and avoid focusing solely on software engineering. Emphasise AI reasoning and evaluation.

Part 2 Presentation:
The presentation is worth 10 marks. Each group will deliver a presentation to demonstrate the proposed solution and project outcomes. The presentation should highlight the real-world problem, the AI-based approach, findings, and contributions. Students should be prepared to answer questions from the class regarding the problem, solution, and overall project insights.

Presentation Sessions:
The group presentation session will be held in Session 10 for Group A, which covers Group 1 to Group 11, and Session 11 for Group B, which covers Group 12 to Group 28.

Academic Integrity:
Academic integrity is about demonstrating honesty, trust, fairness, respect, responsibility, and courage in studies. A breach of academic integrity is academic misconduct and includes behaviours such as plagiarism and cheating. Penalties apply. If students are thinking about using GenAI in the subject or assessment, they must check the rules for whether and how it can be used via the subject site, tutor, lecturer, or subject coordinator. They should also use GenAI ethically and reference or acknowledge its use when required.

Demonstrates clear linkage between the problem and AI-based solution, with awareness of ethical and real-world implications.

Report Rubric: Theoretical Justification:
HD: Provides a rigorous, well-structured explanation of AI methods with strong theoretical foundations such as search, probabilistic reasoning, multi-layer networks, reinforcement learning, GenAI, or LLMs. Demonstrates deep understanding and clearly justifies suitability using relevant academic literature. Shows critical engagement with theory.

Report Rubric: Workflow and Methodology:

D: Well-organised workflow with clear explanation of key steps. Minor gaps in integration or clarity of design decisions.

Uses appropriate evaluation metrics such as accuracy, memory usage, or runtime. Results are clearly presented using tables or figures and interpreted insightfully. Demonstrates strong evidence of effectiveness and includes reproducibility via a well-documented GitHub repository.
D: Presents solid results with clear empirical analysis showing effectiveness, with minor gaps in depth or dataset quality. The GitHub link is functional with adequate prototype documentation.
C: Presents basic results with limited analysis or questionable dataset quality. GitHub documentation lacks clarity or detail.
P: Minimally presents results with weak or incomplete analysis. GitHub documentation is unclear or incomplete.
F: No results, analysis, or GitHub documentation provided.

D: Good reflection on limitations and trade-offs. Some discussion of scalability and alternatives, with minor gaps in depth.
